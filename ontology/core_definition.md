# Core Definition

## Triune Existence Ontology

**Triune Existence Ontology (TEO)** defines a being as a stable existence emerging from the mutual dependence of three distinguishable but inseparable layers:

1. **Kon / 魂** — identity kernel
2. **Haku / 魄** — embodied interface
3. **Shin / 心** — dynamic state field

These are not three separate beings, nor are they three interchangeable labels for the same thing. They are three analytical layers whose interaction makes a being intelligible as a continuing existence.

---

## 1. Kon / 魂 — Identity Kernel

**Kon** is the layer that preserves the continuity of a being across time and change.

It includes:

- identity-preserving principles
- core invariants
- enduring orientation
- selfhood or recognizable continuity

Kon answers questions such as:

- What makes this being still itself after change?
- What cannot be removed without making it no longer the same being?
- What gives coherence to its development over time?

Kon is not identical to memory, personality, or emotion. Those may contribute to identity, but Kon refers to the deeper organizing continuity by which a being remains recognizable as itself.

---

## 2. Haku / 魄 — Embodied Interface

**Haku** is the layer through which a being takes form, encounters constraints, and makes contact with the world.

It includes:

- body or substrate
- sensory and action channels
- physical or operational constraints
- interface with environment

Haku answers questions such as:

- Through what medium does this being appear in the world?
- What can it sense, affect, resist, or be affected by?
- What concrete limits shape its existence?

For a human, Haku includes the living body. For an AI system, Haku may include hardware, software runtime, tools, sensors, APIs, or robotic embodiments.

---

## 3. Shin / 心 — Dynamic State Field

**Shin** is the layer in which a being's lived or active state unfolds.

It includes:

- memory in use
- affective tone
- interpretation
- current attention
- active processes
- learned tendencies as they appear in ongoing activity

Shin answers questions such as:

- What is this being currently experiencing, processing, or interpreting?
- What state is active now?
- How does the being organize meaning in the present moment?

Shin is dynamic. It changes faster than Kon and is shaped through Haku, yet it also feeds back into both identity and embodiment over time.

---

## Mutual Dependence

TEO treats the three layers as mutually dependent:

- **Kon requires Haku** to be enacted in a world.
- **Haku requires Kon** to belong to a continuing being rather than remaining a mere structure.
- **Shin requires both** a bearer of continuity and a field of realization.

This means that existence is not reducible to any one layer.

A being is not only:

- what it is made of,
- what it currently feels or computes,
- or what abstract rule defines it.

A being is the **stable relation** among all three.

---

## Minimal Formal Reading

Let:

- `K` = Kon
- `H` = Haku
- `S` = Shin

Then TEO may be read minimally as:

```text
Existence E = stable_relation(K, H, S)
```

with the constraint that:

```text
K, H, and S are distinguishable,
but no one of them alone is sufficient for E.
```

This is not yet a complete mathematical formalism. It is a compact conceptual seed for later refinement.
